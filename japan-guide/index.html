Tokyo Area Guide

Six essential neighborhoods to explore

Asakusa 🛕
浅草

Old Tokyo. Senso-ji temple, Nakamise shopping street, traditional crafts and street food. The most "traditional Japan" feel in Tokyo. Great for morning visits — less crowded.

🍜 Food Scene 🛍️ Crafts ⛩️ Temples 📸 Photography
Shibuya 🚦
渋谷

The famous scramble crossing, Shibuya 109, Shibuya Stream. Youth fashion, nightlife, massive Tsutaya Bookstore (Starbucks inside). Shibuya Sky observation deck is stunning.

🛍️ Shopping 🌃 Nightlife 📸 Iconic Crossing 🏙️ Observation Deck
Shinjuku 🌆
新宿

Tokyo's busiest hub. Kabukicho entertainment district, Golden Gai (tiny bars), Omoide Yokocho (Memory Lane), Isetan department store. Also the main hub for day trips (Mt. Fuji buses).

🍺 Golden Gai 🛍️ Isetan 🌉 Night Views 🚌 Day Trips
Harajuku 🌸
原宿

Takeshita Street for weird street fashion. Omotesando is the upscale version — luxury boutiques in beautiful buildings. Meiji Shrine is a peaceful forested escape nearby.

👗 Street Fashion ☕ Cafes 🏛️ Luxury Brands ⛩️ Meiji Shrine
Akihabara 🎮
秋葉原

Electric Town. Anime, manga, retro video games, electronics, maid cafes. Best place to find vintage Famicom games, rare electronics, and figure collectibles.

🎮 Gaming 📺 Anime/Manga 💻 Electronics 🧁 Maid Cafes
Ginza 💎
銀座

Tokyo's most upscale district. Flagship luxury stores, galleries, Ginza Six mall. Tsukiji fish market is a short walk. On weekends, Chuo-dori closes to cars — great for a stroll.

💰 Luxury 🐟 Tsukiji Nearby 🎨 Art Galleries 🚶 Weekend Walk

Shopping Tips

Practical knowledge for navigating Japan

🚇 IC Card (Suica / Pasmo)

  • Buy at any JR station ticket machine or airport — just get a Suica
  • Tap in/out at every train, subway, and bus gate
  • Also works at convenience stores, vending machines, some taxis
  • ¥500 deposit — refunded when you return the card
  • Top up at any green ticket machine (JR) or blue (Tokyo Metro)
  • Mobile Suica: add to Apple Wallet (iPhone) — works the same way
  • ⚠️ Physical Suica cards have been in short supply — Mobile Suica is the better option

🧾 Tax-Free Shopping (免税)

  • Save 10% VAT on purchases over ¥5,000 at one store on one day
  • Look for the 免税 sign or "Tax Free" sticker on storefronts
  • Show your passport — they'll attach a form to it (don't remove before you leave)
  • Consumables (food, cosmetics) need ¥5,001+ and must leave Japan sealed
  • Non-consumables (clothes, electronics) have lower minimums at some stores
  • Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Yodobashi, Don Quijote all participate
  • Must export within 30 days of purchase

💴 Cash vs Card Culture

  • Cash is still king — many restaurants and smaller shops are cash only
  • Always keep ¥5,000–10,000 in cash on you
  • 7-Eleven ATMs accept foreign cards with 0 ATM fee from 7-Eleven
  • Japan Post bank ATMs also work reliably with Visa/MC
  • IC Cards (Suica) work at konbini (convenience stores) everywhere
  • PayPay is Japan's dominant QR app — you can use it with a foreign card
  • Hotels, department stores, and chains: cards usually accepted
  • Tabelog 100 restaurants: many are cash only — check before you go

💡 General Japan Tips

  • Convenience stores (konbini) are incredible — hot food, ATMs, printing, coffee
  • Onigiri at 7-Eleven: ¥130-180 and genuinely great. Try tuna mayo.
  • Rubbish bins are rare on streets — carry a small bag for trash
  • Shoes come off in traditional restaurants, temples, ryokan — wear nice socks
  • Quiet on trains — no phone calls, keep voice low
  • Tipping is NOT a thing — it can actually be offensive
  • Google Maps works great for transit directions with real-time updates

🍜 Restaurant Tips

  • Tabelog is the Japanese Yelp — scores above 3.5 are very good, 4.0+ is exceptional
  • Tabelog 100 list: the 100 best restaurants in Japan — use the list
  • Many top spots are cash only — ask/check before ordering
  • Lunch sets (ランチセット) are often 50-60% cheaper than dinner
  • Vending machine at restaurant entrance = order before sitting (ramen shops)
  • Oshibori (hot towel) when seated — wipe hands only, not face
  • Kanpai! (乾杯) — clink glasses and say it before drinking
